About

I am an Associate Professor whose work brings together design anthropology, science and technology studies, participatory design, and human-computer interaction. My research investigates how people navigate uncertainty, transformation, and competing visions of social and technological futures.

Current projects explore questions of transition, climate imaginaries, aging, temporal experience, and the politics of future-making. I am particularly interested in overlooked practices, alternative narratives, and situated forms of knowledge that challenge dominant accounts of progress and innovation.

Research Themes

  • Transition Design and Sociotechnical Change
  • Alternative Imaginaries and Future-Making
  • Temporalities, Endings, and Transformation
  • Aging, Care, and Everyday Life
  • Design Anthropology and Participatory Research
How are futures made imaginable, and what forms of collective work are required to make them livable?

Current Projects

Recent work includes TRANSIT: Temporal Resistance, Alternative Narratives and Situated Imaginaries of Transition, as well as research on endings, temporal infrastructures, and collaborative methods for engaging uncertain futures.
